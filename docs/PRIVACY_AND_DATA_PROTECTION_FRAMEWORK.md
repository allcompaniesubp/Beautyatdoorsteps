# BeautyDoorstep — Draft Privacy and Data Protection Framework

**Status: planning draft for privacy counsel and security review. Not a final privacy notice.** Actual purposes, legal bases, controllers, processors, retention periods and user rights depend on the operator and each launch jurisdiction.

## 1. Scope and responsible entity

This framework covers the planned BeautyDoorstep website, directory and marketplace. Before launch, identify the legal entity responsible for the service, its registered address, privacy contact and any local representative or grievance contact required by law.

## 2. Data minimisation

Collect only information needed for a defined feature. Potential data may include account details, business listing data, transaction records, provider credentials, technical security logs and optional location data. Health, legal matter or identity information should be collected only if a separately approved feature truly requires it.

Do not collect Aadhaar, passport, tax IDs, medical records, criminal-history data or other sensitive documents merely to create a directory account.

## 3. Purpose, lawful basis and choice

For every data field, document the purpose, lawful basis, whether it is optional, recipients, retention period and deletion path. Obtain consent where required and make withdrawal as easy as giving consent. Do not bundle marketing consent with essential service terms.

## 4. Sensitive professional and government documents

Store documents only in private, access-controlled storage. Use encryption in transit and at rest, short-lived access links, least-privilege roles, audit logs, malware scanning, retention schedules and secure deletion. Never place document images, identity numbers or health records in public URLs, public profile fields, analytics events or source code.

Professional verification states should include draft, pending, rejected, expired and approved. A badge must accurately state what was checked, by whom and when. A platform check is not a government credential or guarantee.

## 5. Health and legal information

Health records and legal matter details require separate feature-specific notices, strict access controls and purpose limitation. Do not use health or legal data for advertising, profiling, unrelated recommendations or sale of leads. Restrict staff access and log every authorised access. Set retention and deletion according to professional, medical-record and legal obligations.

## 6. Location and tracking

Ask for location permission only when needed and explain why. Offer manual address or area selection where practical. Precise live location is shared only with the relevant assigned provider and support roles for the active job/trip, and automatically stops when it ends. Retain only the minimum event or dispute evidence required by law and disclose that retention separately.

## 7. Sharing and processors

Disclose categories of recipients such as hosting, database, payment, maps, messaging, identity-check and customer-support vendors. Execute required data-processing agreements, assess security and international transfers, and do not sell personal information. Providers receive only the information needed to fulfil the specific booking or order.

Government integrations require the agency/API programme's authorisation, approved purposes, consent or other lawful basis, security controls and audit requirements. Never share user documents with a government body merely because a listing is present.

## 8. Cookies, analytics and marketing

List each cookie or SDK, purpose, provider, duration and opt-out. Non-essential analytics and advertising technologies should remain off until valid consent is obtained where required. Do not send sensitive health, legal, identity or precise-location values to analytics or ad platforms.

## 9. Security and incident response

Use strong authentication, role-based access, multi-factor authentication for administrators, secrets management, encryption, rate limits, backups, monitoring, vulnerability management and tested incident-response procedures. Never expose database service-role keys in client code or public repositories.

Maintain a breach-response plan covering containment, investigation, regulator/user notification and evidence preservation within applicable legal deadlines.

## 10. Retention and user rights

Create a retention schedule by data type. Provide workflows for access, correction, export, objection, restriction, deletion and consent withdrawal where legally required. Some records may need to be retained for tax, safety, fraud, professional or legal claims; explain the basis and duration. Deletion must propagate to processors where required.

## 11. Children and vulnerable users

Do not knowingly onboard children into services that require adult consent. Add age gates, guardian workflows and safeguarding rules where the service category requires them. High-risk categories such as childcare, healthcare and elder care require specialist safeguarding review.

## 12. International operations

Before opening a country, map local privacy laws, data residency or localisation rules, cross-border transfer mechanisms, breach duties, cookie rules, consumer protections and regulator contacts. Do not assume one global privacy notice is sufficient.

## 13. Required implementation register

For each feature, maintain a data inventory with: field name, purpose, sensitivity, lawful basis, controller/processor, recipients, storage region, retention, deletion mechanism, security owner and user-facing notice. Complete a privacy impact assessment for high-risk processing such as precise tracking, health data, identity verification and large-scale profiling.

## 14. Contact

Insert the operating entity's privacy email, postal address, data-protection officer or grievance contact (where required) and regulator complaint routes before launch.
